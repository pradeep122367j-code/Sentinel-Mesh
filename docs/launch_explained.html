What happens when you click Launch

Each step below has two sides. The left side is what people will see on screen. The right side is what you can say out loud, in plain words, to an audience that has never heard of any of this.

The one sentence to start with

"Launch starts a pretend cyber attack inside our system. It feeds the attack in as alerts, and then we watch our team of AI agents investigate it and ask a human, Maya, before doing anything risky."

Keep these three ideas and you can explain anything else

  • Alert = a warning that something looks odd on the network.
  • Agent = a small AI worker with one job (sort, investigate, or propose a fix).
  • Approval gate = the AI is never allowed to take a risky action alone. A human clicks Approve or Reject.

Before the click: the Scenarios page

  • Three cards are shown. Each is a short story: Phishing to lateral movement, Vendor-dependency CVE, and Malicious open-source package.
  • Each card has a title, a summary, the list of agents it uses, and a blue Launch scenario button.
  • The badge on the card says not started.
  • Only an analyst like Maya sees the button. A viewer sees a "read-only" banner and no button. Say this: "People who should only look cannot press the dangerous buttons."
The whole journey in one line
Click Launch→ Alerts arrive→ Triage→ Investigate→ Propose a fix→ Human approves→ Action runs→ Audit log

Main demo: "Phishing to lateral movement"

This is the best one to present. It is a story anyone understands: one employee clicks a bad link and the attacker starts moving through the company.

1

You click "Launch scenario"

On screen
  • The button greys out straight away so it cannot be clicked twice.
  • The page waits for a moment while the system works.
  • The card badge changes from not started to N waiting for you.
  • The card now says "Launched … ago by Maya" and lists three incidents underneath.
Say this
  • "I pressed one button. Behind it, the system just created three alerts, like three alarm bells."
  • "Each alert is one small clue of the same attack."
  • "The card now records who launched it and when. Nothing here is anonymous."

3

Agent 1, Triage: "Is this worth a human's time?"

On screen (open an incident → Triage box)
  • Decision: escalate or dismiss.
  • Severity and Confidence numbers.
  • Anomaly score: how strange this traffic is.
  • Technique: an ID from MITRE ATT&CK, a public catalogue of known attack methods.
  • A written reason underneath.
Say this
  • "Think of Triage as the hospital front desk nurse. Most alerts are harmless and get dismissed. These three were escalated."
  • "It also tells you why, and how sure it is. No black box."
  • "This is how we cut alert noise so people only see what matters."
4

Agent 2, Investigation: "What exactly happened?"

On screen (Investigation box)
  • A short written summary of the attack.
  • A numbered list of claims. Each claim has small reference codes next to it.
  • An Evidence section: the raw log lines those codes point to.
  • The text "every claim cited".
Say this
  • "Investigation is the detective. It writes a short report."
  • "Every sentence points to the actual log line that proves it. If it cannot cite evidence, it cannot claim it."
  • "That is how we stop the AI from making things up."
  • Also point at "Injection scan" on the alert: "We check that attackers cannot talk to our AI through the data. Any hidden text is shown as plain, harmless text."
5

Agent 3, Containment: "What should we do about it?"

On screen (Actions box and the Approval queue page)
  • One proposed action per alert, such as Isolate host → ws-fin-07 or Block IP → 10.20.0.5.
  • Each action is marked gated, meaning a human must approve it.
  • A status of "awaiting approval". The Approval queue page lists all of them with a Review and decide button.
Say this
  • "Containment is the planner. It proposes the fix but it cannot do it by itself."
  • "Isolating a laptop means cutting it off the network. Doing that by mistake could stop someone's work, so a human must agree first."
  • "The queue is Maya's to-do list of decisions."
6

The Human Approval Gate: Maya decides

On screen (yellow-tinted decision box at the top of the incident)
  • Title: Human Approval Gate.
  • The action, the reason, who proposed it, trust tier, whether it is destructive, and the attached evidence.
  • A note box, then green Approve and red Reject buttons, and "Recorded as maya…".
Say this
  • "Here is where the AI stops and waits. Everything Maya needs is on one screen: what, why, and the proof."
  • "Her note and her name are saved permanently. If anyone asks later who approved this, the answer is here."

8

Audit log: the proof trail

On screen (Audit log page, and Timeline at the bottom of an incident)
  • A time-ordered list of everything that happened, each entry naming the actor.
  • On the incident: Timing and integrity shows detect time, contain time, time spent waiting on a human, and a green badge chain verified.
Say this
  • "Every step is written into a tamper-evident log. Each entry is linked to the one before, like a chain. Change one and the chain visibly breaks."
  • "Green means nobody altered the history. That is what auditors and regulators ask for."
  • "The timings show how quickly we detected and contained the attack."

Be honest about these, they will come up

"Is this a real attack?"No. Launch replays scripted stories. The alerts are simulated so the demo is repeatable.
"Did it really isolate a computer?"No. Actions run against a simulated connector. The flow, rules, approvals and refusals are real, but nothing in a real network is touched.
"Can the AI act alone?"Not on anything risky. Every risky action waits at the approval gate.
"Why can I only launch each one once?"Each story is a one-time run, so the record stays clean. After launching, the button shows "Launched".
"What if the AI is wrong?"Every claim shows its evidence and confidence, and a human decides. Mistakes can be caught and rejected.

Closing line

"One click started a full attack story. AI agents sorted it, investigated it with proof, and proposed fixes. A human made the final call, the system refused one unsafe action on its own, and every step was recorded so it can be checked later."
